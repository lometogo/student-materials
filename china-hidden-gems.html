Hidden Gems in China

Where the Guidebooks Don't Go

Whenever people talk about traveling in China, the same places usually come up: Beijing, Shanghai, Xi'an, Chengdu, and Zhangjiajie. They're all on my list, but I'm more curious about the places that don't get nearly as much attention.

I'm talking about the small towns, scenic areas, local neighborhoods, or even a random street you stumbled across that ended up being more memorable than the famous attractions. Sometimes those unexpected places are what make a trip special.

I've heard people mention places like Nanxun Ancient Town, Dongji Islands, and parts of Yunnan, but I'm sure there are countless hidden gems that most international travelers have never heard of.

If you could recommend just one hidden gem in China, what would it be, and why? I'd love to add a few lesser-known places to my itinerary instead of only visiting the famous tourist spots.

Jiangxi Province as a whole is underrated. Spicier food than Sichuan/Chongqing, lots of beautiful nature, lakes river, and mountain landscapes.

Honestly, one of my favorite "hidden gems" is not a hidden village in the mountains, but a place that many international visitors skip: Jingshan (near Hangzhou).

Most people only know West Lake when they visit Hangzhou, but Jingshan feels completely different. It's quieter, surrounded by tea fields and forests, and has a very local atmosphere.

There is an old temple on the mountain with a long history, but what I like most is the feeling of slowing down there — walking through the tea gardens, having a cup of local tea, and seeing a side of China that feels less "tour group" and more everyday life.

Another place I'd mention is Taizhou in Zhejiang. It's not as famous internationally, but it has amazing coastal scenery, old villages, and temples. Places like this are where you sometimes get the best memories because you're not just checking off famous attractions.

China is huge, and honestly some of my favorite moments are not from the "top 10 places", but from random small towns, local restaurants, and neighborhoods where you just slow down :)

It's not really hidden but I personally haven't seen that many reels of westerners talking about Jiuzai Valley (Nine Villages Valley) in north Sichuan. Idk if it's just my algorithm.

I've heard the scenery changed a bit after the earthquakes. So it's been a loooong time since I went there. But it was such an ethereal place rivaling Zhangjiajie for me. I'm curious if anyone who's been to both recently can provide an updated comparison?

I snuck passed a do not enter sign up the roof of Yangtze River boat between Jiangyin and Nanjing. Spent the night under a breeze watching sparks out of the chimney.

Watching the sunset and having tea with my partner at a residence that used to be Chiang Kai-shek and his wife's vacation house overlooking Taihu Lake in Yuantouzhu Scenic Area of Wuxi

Shanxi Province boasts the largest and best-preserved ancient architectural complex in China. Ancient Shanxi was one of the wealthiest provinces in China, but later became one of the poorest. Consequently, many ancient buildings have been preserved to this day due to a lack of funding.

If you are lucky enough, you might have the opportunity to visit some national-level cultural relics that have been closed to the public for centuries. (This is because there are very few foreign tourists, and local staff may be willing to make an exception and allow you a tour.)

Chongqing has so many great spots that are just a bullet train away. Ayi river and juili ancient town are AMAZING, for instance

Migrant neighborhoods in Guangzhou. Its like going to another planet.

Dulongjiang, the most remote valley in Yunnan, pressed up against the borders of Tibet and Myanmar, the headwaters of the Irrawaddy River, only accessible 6 months of the year (then, before the second tunnel was built under Gaoligongshan)

Hanzhong, Shaanxi- an ancient Buddhist temple way up a mountain outside town, with a Taoist temple near by.

Last time when in China, went to Niangzian. Fully restored part of the Great Wall, but best part was the walk from the Great Wall along the river to a small town. You can tell it is a tourist area for the locals. PS, the food is awesome
