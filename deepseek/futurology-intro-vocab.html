The institutions1 we've inherited2 weren't built for a world this fast, this connected. While the institute is optimistic3 about where technology can take us, we are also very aware of the dystopias4 it can construct5. Here on Futurology, we're doing the work of naming what's next, of imagining a future we can accomplish together instead of one that we must all work to prevent.

In this episode, Nicholas and Nathan look back on what they saw, what they've built, and how they've stayed focused on designing institutions that meet the pace and complexity6 of the world we now live in. And now, our own Nicholas Brewan and Nathan Gardell on Futurology.

Vocabulary

↑ 1. Institutions: (in-stuh-TOO-shuhnz) - established organizations or systems that shape how society works; organizations, establishments. Banks, schools, and courts are all institutions we rely on every day.
↑ 2. Inherited: (in-HAIR-it-ed) - received from the past or from earlier generations; handed down, passed on. We inherited this way of working from our grandparents' generation.
↑ 3. Optimistic: (op-tuh-MISS-tik) - hopeful and confident about the future; positive, upbeat. She remained optimistic that the project would succeed.
↑ 4. Dystopias: (dis-TOH-pee-uhz) - imagined societies in which life is extremely bad, unfair, or frightening; nightmare worlds, oppressive futures. The novel describes a dystopia controlled by machines.
↑ 5. Construct: (kuhn-STRUHKT) - to build, create, or form something; build, create, form. Engineers construct bridges designed to last for decades.
↑ 6. Complexity: (kuhm-PLEK-suh-tee) - the state of being complicated and made of many connected parts; intricacy, complication. The complexity of the system makes it difficult to change.
