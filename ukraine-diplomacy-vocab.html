Trump's Diplomacy Gap: Ukraine, Iran, and Two Men in the Middle

Vocabulary study prepared by Terence Collins  ·  Business English Coach

At the center of the disconnect1 between the increasing violence in Eastern Europe and the Trump administration's seeming lack of focus on it are the president's two main negotiators for both Iran and Ukraine: the special envoy2 Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, President Trump's son-in-law. They traveled to Qatar this week for another round of Iran talks, even as Ukraine was launching more drones toward Moscow and as Russia was readying its latest barrage3 targeting Kyiv, where at least 21 people were reported killed Thursday in overnight attacks.

The last time Russian and Ukrainian officials are known to have met face to face was in Switzerland in February, with Mr. Witkoff and Mr. Kushner sitting between them. In the months since, the Iran war consumed4 the Trump officials' attention, even as the fighting escalated5 between Russia and Ukraine — a conflict that Mr. Trump had promised to solve within 24 hours.

"We were focused on Iran," Mr. Trump said last month, suggesting that the administration could renew its efforts to end the Ukraine war once Iran was "in the rearview mirror."

The limited diplomatic bandwidth6 at Mr. Trump's disposal has highlighted his extraordinarily slimmed-down7 approach to high-stakes8 diplomacy9. The post of U.S. ambassador to Moscow has been vacant10 for more than a year, and the acting ambassador to Kyiv resigned in April. Secretary of State Marco Rubio and other top diplomats have played a limited role on Ukraine, leaving Mr. Trump's two envoys as the pivotal11 players.

Vocabulary Definitions

  1. 1. Disconnect: (dis-kuh-NEKT) - a gap or mismatch between two things that should be related or aligned; gap, mismatch, inconsistency. There was a clear disconnect between what the manager promised and what the team actually delivered. ↑ back
  2. 2. Envoy: (EN-voy) - an official representative or messenger sent by a government to handle a specific diplomatic mission; diplomat, representative, emissary. The president appointed a special envoy to lead the peace negotiations. ↑ back
  3. 3. Barrage: (buh-RAHZH) - a concentrated and overwhelming outpouring of something, often attacks, criticism, or questions; bombardment, volley, onslaught. The company faced a barrage of complaints after the product recall. ↑ back
  4. 4. Consumed (as used here): (kun-YOOMD) - to completely absorb or dominate someone's time, attention, or energy; absorbed, dominated, occupied. The merger consumed the entire leadership team for six months. ↑ back
  5. 5. Escalated: (ES-kuh-lay-ted) - increased in intensity, severity, or scope, often rapidly; intensified, worsened, expanded. What began as a minor disagreement quickly escalated into a serious dispute. ↑ back
  6. 6. Diplomatic bandwidth: (dip-luh-MAT-ik BAND-width) - the capacity, time, and human resources a government has available to conduct international negotiations and relations; diplomatic capacity, negotiating resources, foreign policy capacity. A country fighting two wars simultaneously will quickly exhaust its diplomatic bandwidth. ↑ back
  7. 7. Slimmed-down: (SLIMD-down) - reduced in size, staff, or scope, often to increase efficiency or cut costs; streamlined, reduced, scaled-back. The company launched a slimmed-down version of the product to meet the tight deadline. ↑ back
  8. 8. High-stakes: (HY-stays) - involving serious consequences or significant risk if the outcome goes wrong; critical, consequential, make-or-break. The board meeting was a high-stakes moment that would determine the company's future. ↑ back
  9. 9. Diplomacy: (dih-PLOH-muh-see) - the professional management of international relations through negotiation, communication, and compromise rather than force; negotiation, statecraft, foreign relations. Skilled diplomacy prevented what could have become a serious trade war between the two nations. ↑ back
  10. 10. Vacant: (VAY-kunt) - empty and unfilled, referring to a position, role, or place that has no one currently occupying it; unfilled, empty, unoccupied. The CFO position had been vacant for three months before they found the right candidate. ↑ back
  11. 11. Pivotal: (PIV-uh-tul) - of critical importance; acting as the central point on which everything else depends; crucial, decisive, key. Her presentation to the board turned out to be the pivotal moment in securing the contract. ↑ back
