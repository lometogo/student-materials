Ace the Interview: The Three-Move Framework

Interview Coaching Recording ~2 min
Listen to the recording
Coach's Note Practice using this many times until you can say it confidently.

Your English doesn't have to be perfect to ace the Western interview.

What you do need is a framework1 or a structure that prepares you for any question they throw at you. Say for instance they ask you "why should we hire you?" You answer in three moves.

"At my last company I turned around2 a failing product launch3 in six weeks, and the product became our biggest seller." That's your proof4.

"I know you guys are expanding fast, and that's where I do my best work." That's where you're the fit5 to the company.

Second thing to remember is to interview the interviewer6. Now it's important of course that you're prepared7, and with this framework, you'll be prepared to answer the questions your interviewer asks you.

But equally important is the questions that you ask the interviewer, either during the course of the interview or at the end.

So make sure you have two or three very good questions to ask. For instance, "What does success look like in this job in 90 days?" Or "What kinds of things does the person taking this job, what are the biggest or what's the biggest challenge8 this job faces in the next six months?" Or "What do your best performers have in common?"

Coach's Note Come up with three questions that relate to the job you're applying for and/or questions about the company, its future plans, challenges and opportunities it faces.

Smart, confident, comfortable questions.

These questions alone or asking questions like them will make you stand out9 from most of the other interviewees they face.

And third, remember, the interviewer is on your side.

Remember, this isn't their real job. They're actually taking time away from their job to do these interviews. It's necessary but they really want to get back to the pressing things10 that are happening in their daily life, in their job. They want you to succeed.

Work with them. Show them that you're the candidate11 through confidence12, a good ability to answer questions, and a comfort level13.

Finally, mindset14.

Breathe, prepare, and expect the best. Would you like to have a full coaching session15 where we go over these points in great detail so that you'll feel prepared when you walk into the interview?

Vocabulary

1. ↩ Framework (FRAME-werk) — a basic structure or set of ideas that supports a plan or system; structure, system, blueprint. She built a framework for evaluating each proposal fairly.
2. ↩ Turned around (turnd uh-ROWND) — changed something from failing to succeeding; revived, reversed, salvaged. The new manager turned around the struggling department in just one quarter.
3. ↩ Launch (lawnch) — the introduction of a new product to the market; release, rollout, debut. The company celebrated the successful launch of its latest app.
4. ↩ Proof (proof) — evidence that something is true or has happened; evidence, confirmation, verification. His sales record was proof that he could handle the role.
5. ↩ Fit (fit) — a good match between a person's skills or style and a job or organization; match, compatibility, alignment. Her collaborative style made her a strong fit for the team.
6. ↩ Interview the interviewer (IN-ter-vyoo thee IN-ter-vyoo-er) — to ask the person conducting the interview thoughtful questions in return, showing engagement and confidence; engage the interviewer, question back. He decided to interview the interviewer by asking about the team's biggest challenges.
7. ↩ Prepared (pri-PAIRD) — ready for something because of planning or practice beforehand; ready, equipped, primed. She felt prepared after rehearsing her answers all week.
8. ↩ Challenge (CHAL-inj) — a difficult task or problem that tests someone's ability; difficulty, obstacle, hurdle. Meeting the tight deadline was the biggest challenge of the project.
9. ↩ Stand out (stand owt) — to be noticeably better or different from others in a group; distinguish yourself, shine, be noticed. Asking thoughtful questions helped her stand out from other candidates.
10. ↩ Pressing things (PRESS-ing things) — urgent matters that need immediate attention; urgent matters, priorities, pressing issues. He had to step away from the meeting to deal with some pressing things at the office.
11. ↩ Candidate (KAN-di-dayt) — a person being considered for a job or position; applicant, contender, nominee. Only three candidates were selected for the final round.
12. ↩ Confidence (KON-fi-dens) — a feeling of self-assurance in one's abilities; self-assurance, certainty, poise. His confidence during the presentation impressed the entire panel.
13. ↩ Comfort level (KUM-fert LEV-el) — the degree to which someone feels relaxed and at ease in a situation; ease, composure, relaxed manner. Practicing beforehand raised her comfort level during the actual interview.
14. ↩ Mindset (MYND-set) — a person's established set of attitudes or way of thinking; attitude, outlook, mentality. A positive mindset helped him stay calm under pressure.
15. ↩ Coaching session (KOHCH-ing SESH-un) — a meeting focused on guiding and training someone to improve a specific skill; training session, mentoring meeting. They scheduled a coaching session to practice answering tough interview questions.
